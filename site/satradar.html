How this works

Every 15 minutes EUMETSAT's Meteosat satellite over the Indian Ocean scans the region, and EUMETSAT's H SAF service turns the infrared and microwave readings into a rain rate (product H63). This page reads those pictures directly, so it refreshes on its own every 15 minutes while open. Each new picture arrives about 45–55 minutes after the scan.

Satellite rain over Tamil Nadu is usually too high, because the cold cloud shields of the monsoon look like rain from space. Compared with the 565 TN SMART gauges on 10–15 Oct 2026, it gave about 3 to 5 times the measured rain, so rates here are multiplied by 0.3. This correction will be tuned against TN SMART as more days come in.

Forecast frames (purple label) move the latest rain areas along their recent track: the page compares the last two pictures 30 minutes apart, works out how each part of the rain is moving, and carries it forward. Rain that forms or dies in the meantime is not predicted, so trust the first hour more than the second. "Now" is itself a forecast, because the newest picture is already 45–55 minutes old.
