How the runs are made

  • Live data each cycle. The latest GFS analysis (the observed state at 00, 06, 12 or 18 UTC; no forecast steps), Bay sea temperature from NOAA OISST (as on your forecast page), and the latest real-time MJO index (NOAA PSL). The seedling, any Arabian Sea low and the south-west Bay circulation are found in that analysis.
  • Winds after the start. Today’s winds at 850, 700, 500 and 200 hPa fade over about 2 days into the real ERA5 history of the 12 past Oct–Dec Bay systems (2000–2025) whose state best matches today. No past storm may carry more than a quarter of the weight.
  • Track. Our steering model, fitted on every Bay storm since 1980: the storm moves with the ring-mean wind 2–6° around it plus the north-west drift, using the weak, moderate or strong version according to its current strength.
  • Strength. Over sea, a 12-hour change model fitted on the 84 past storms (current wind, last change, age, latitude, 200–850 hPa shear), plus hand-set nudges for sea temperature (+1.5 kt per 12 h per °C above the library average) and MJO (±1 kt per 12 h). Over land, the Kaplan–DeMaria inland decay.
  • 51 combinations. Member 0 has every input at today’s value. Members 1–27 are an L27 orthogonal array over the 13 inputs (each pair of settings of any two inputs together exactly 3 times); members 28–50 are a second, shifted block of the same array. Start position and formation time are spread a little inside each setting (fixed seed per member).
